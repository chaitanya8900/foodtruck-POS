1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY  
1.1 Product Overview  
The proposed product is an integrated Point of Sale (POS), QR Code Self -Ordering, 
Billing, Payment Management and Kitchen Order Ticket (KOT) Management System 
designed initially for a food truck.  
The system will allow customers to order food through two different channels:  
1. Counter Ordering:  Customers place orders directly with the cashier, who enters 
the items into the POS system.  
2. QR Code Self -Ordering:  Customers scan a QR code placed on their table or at 
the food truck, browse the digital menu, add items to their cart and submit their 
orders using their mobile phones.  
Both ordering channels will connect to one centralized backend and database. All 
orders will follow a common order -processing, payment, token -generation and printing 
workflow.  
The system will use a single thermal printer installed at the billing counter.  
For every confirmed order, the printer will print two consecutive slips:  
1. Customer Token / Receipt.  
2. Kitchen Order Ticket (KOT).  
The printer will automatically cut the paper between the two slips. The cashier will hand 
the customer token to the customer and physically deliver the KOT to the kitchen.  
The system will also provide an administrative dashboard for managing menus, prices, 
orders, payments, tables, sales reports and other business operations.  
1.2 Business Objectives  

=== PAGE 2 ===
The primary objectives are:  
• Digitize the food truck's complete ordering and billing process.  
• Eliminate manual handwritten bills and kitchen tickets.  
• Provide customers with a convenient QR -based ordering experience.  
• Reduce the time required to place and process orders.  
• Maintain a single centralized order database.  
• Prevent unpaid orders from entering kitchen preparation.  
• Automate customer token and KOT printing.  
• Maintain accurate sales and payment records.  
• Provide the owner with visibility into daily business operations.  
• Build an architecture that can support additional food trucks or restaurant 
locations in the future.  
1.3 Key Product Principles  
The product must follow these principles:  
1. Every order, regardless of its source, must be stored in the same database.  
2. Counter and QR ordering must use the same menu and pricing information.  
3. No order should enter the kitchen workflow without payment confirmation.  
4. Every confirmed order must have a unique customer -facing token.  
5. Customer token and KOT must contain the same order/token reference.  
6. Only one thermal printer is required in the initial deployment.  
7. Printing failures must not result in lost orders.  
8. Payment verification must happen securely on the backend.  
9. The system must be easy enough for non -technical food truck staff to operate.  
10. The customer QR ordering experience must work without installing a mobile 
application.

7. SYSTEM MODULE 4 – PAYMENT MANAGEMENT  
7.1 Payment Types  
The application must support:  
• Cash.  
• UPI at counter.  
• Prepaid online UPI through a payment gateway.  
The system must distinguish payment initiation from successful payment.  
7.2 Prepaid UPI Workflow  
1. Customer creates a QR order.  
2. Backend creates an order with payment status Pending.  
3. Backend initiates a payment request through the payment gateway.  
4. Customer completes UPI payment.  
5. Payment gateway communicates payment status to the backend.  
6. Backend verifies the payment signature and transaction status.  
7. Successful payment is recorded.  
8. Order payment status becomes Paid.  
9. Order becomes eligible for token issuance.  
10. Order appears in the Mobile Orders dashboard.  
11. Cashier clicks Generate Token & Print.  
12. Backend assigns a token.  
13. Customer token and KOT are printed.  
The backend must not trust payment -success information received only from the 
customer's browser.  

=== PAGE 18 ===
Payment webhooks must be verified.  
7.3 Pay -at-Counter Workflow  
1. Customer creates a QR order.  
2. Customer selects Pay at Counter.  
3. Order is saved.  
4. Payment status becomes Pending.  
5. Order status becomes Pending Payment.  
6. No token is issued.  
7. No KOT is printed.  
8. Order appears in Mobile Orders.  
9. Customer reaches the billing counter.  
10. Cashier checks the order.  
11. Cashier collects payment.  
12. Cashier selects Cash or UPI.  
13. Cashier confirms payment.  
14. Backend records the payment.  
15. Order status becomes Confirmed.  
16. Token is generated.  
17. Both slips are printed.  
7.4 Payment Failure  
If prepaid UPI fails:  
• The order must not be confirmed.  
• The kitchen must not receive a KOT.  
• The customer should see a payment failure/pending message.  
• The customer may retry payment within the configured payment window.  
• The system must prevent multiple successful payments from creating duplicate 
orders.

9. SYSTEM MODULE 6 – TOKEN MANAGEMENT  
9.1 Token Generation  
Each confirmed order must receive a unique customer -facing token.  
Token generation must happen on the backend.  
Tokens must not be generated by the browser.  

=== PAGE 22 ===
9.2 Token Format  
Recommended format:  
001, 002, 003, 004  
Alternatively:  
A001, A002, A003  
The format should be configurable.  
9.3 Token Number Rules  
• Tokens must be unique within the configured numbering period.  
• Concurrent orders must not receive the same token.  
• Pending -payment orders must not consume a token.  
• Failed payment orders must not consume a token.  
• Tokens should be assigned only when an order is confirmed and ready for 
kitchen processing.  
• Reprinting must not create a new token.  
• Cancelled tokens must not be reassigned to another order.  
• Token numbering may reset at the start of a business day.  
The business -day reset must account for the food truck's operating hours and timezone.  
9.4 Token and Internal Order ID  
The application must maintain two identifiers:  
Internal Order ID  
• Unique database identifier.  
• Used for APIs and database relationships.  
Customer Token Number  
• Short human -readable identifier.  
• Printed on the receipt and KOT.  
• Used by staff to identify the order.  
The KOT and customer token must always reference the same order.

18. SECURITY REQUIREMENTS  
Security must be considered from the beginning.  
18.1 Authentication  
• Passwords must be hashed.  
• Authentication sessions must be protected.  
• Admin and cashier permissions must be enforced on the backend.  
• Inactive users must not be able to log in.  
18.2 Authorization  
Every protected API must validate the user's role.  
A cashier must not be able to access admin -only operations by manually calling an API.  
18.3 Payment Security  
• Verify gateway webhooks.  
• Validate payment signatures.  
• Use idempotency keys.  
• Do not trust frontend payment status.  
• Do not store UPI PINs, card PINs or sensitive payment credentials.  
• Store payment references rather than unnecessary sensitive payment 
information.  
18.4 QR Security  
• Use unique random QR identifiers.  
• Avoid exposing internal database IDs.  
• Validate table identifiers on the backend.  
• Apply rate limits to public ordering APIs.  
• Prevent duplicate order submissions.  
18.5 Printer Security  

=== PAGE 46 ===
• Use authenticated printer agents.  
• Use secure outbound connections.  
• Do not expose printer ports publicly.  
• Restrict printer commands to approved print jobs.  
18.6 Audit Logging

• User ID.  
• Activity.  
• Entity affected.

26. FINAL PRODUCT DEFINITION  
The final product will be a unified food truck management platform with two 
independent order -entry interfaces and a common order -processing system.  
Counter POS  
Allows staff to manually enter customer orders, collect payments, generate tokens and 
print receipts and KOTs.  
Customer QR Ordering  
Allows customers to scan QR codes, browse the menu, place dine -in or takeaway 
orders and pay through prepaid UPI or at the counter.  
Mobile Orders Management  
Allows the cashier to monitor incoming QR orders, confirm counter payments, issue 
tokens and initiate printing.  
Centralized Order Management  
Maintains all counter and QR orders in one database with consistent order, payment, 
token and print statuses.  
Single Thermal Printer  
Prints the customer token and KOT consecutively, cutting the paper between them.  
Admin Dashboard  
Allows the owner to manage the menu, prices, business settings, users, orders, 
payments, reports and printer configuration.  
The product must prioritize simplicity, reliability, fast order processing, payment 
accuracy and uninterrupted printing.  
The initial release should focus on delivering a dependable single -location food truck 
POS system. The underlying architecture should be sufficiently modular to support 
multiple locations and additional restaurant -management capabilities in future 
releases .

The integration must use PhonePe's official Payment Gateway APIs and documentation.  
Expected workflow:  
1. Customer selects UPI.

3. Backend validates the order and calculates the final amount.  
4. Backend creates a unique payment transaction reference.

12. Order becomes eligible for token generation.

• Payment status must be independently verifiable through the payment status

• Duplicate webhook requests must not create duplicate payments or orders.  
• Payment requests must use unique merchant transaction identifiers.  
• Failed or pending payments must not enter the confirmed kitchen workflow.  
• The backend must handle delayed payment confirmations.  
2.4 Payment Failure Handling

• Order must remain unconfirmed.  
• Payment status must be Failed, or Pending if the result is not yet definitive.  
• No token should be issued.  
• No KOT should be printed.

Important implementation notes  
There are three technical details I would particularly emphasize to the 
development team:  
1. One QR, two order types: The QR identifies the table. The customer 
independently chooses Dine -in or Takeaway. Keep tableId, qrTokenId and 
orderType separate in the database.  
2. PhonePe integration: Use PhonePe's current official Payment Gateway 
integration documentation when implementing the checkout. The exact API flow, 
SDK and payment modes should be finalized against the merchant account's 
enabled capabilities. The customer -facing payment choices remain UPI and Pay at 
Counter.  
3. POS popup timing: The cashier selects payment method, then chooses Dine -in 
or Takeaway in the final confirmation popup. No token or print job should be 
generated until both are confirmed.  
One small recommendation: Make the order -type selection popup visually 
prominent, with two large cards rather than a small dropdown. This is an important 
operational choice, and the cashier or customer should not accidentally skip it.
